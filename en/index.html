01 About

A developer with a designer's eye

I'm Seyed Mohammad Ghaznavi — a .NET developer with a designer's eye. For me, the worlds of code and design have never been separate; they share a common language: attention to detail.

On one side, I build enterprise web applications — with ASP.NET, layered architecture, LINQ, and code that's readable and maintainable. I love refactoring — that moment when messy code becomes clean, lasting structure. I think analytically and logically; I prefer to fully understand a problem before writing code.

On the other side, I spend hours in Blender, Inkscape, and GIMP. I model in 3D, set up scene lighting, and design logos and visual identities. The tools I've chosen are free and legal — not out of limitation, but because I believe when you're building something meant to last for years, it should be clean and legitimate from the foundation.

I'm quiet and calm by nature, and I prefer to let my work speak for itself. Over all these years I've learned one thing: quality isn't a matter of tools; it's a matter of eye and conscience.

05 Principle

Free tools, a clear conscience

All of my work is produced with free and legal software: Blender for 3D, Inkscape for vector and logo, GIMP for image editing, DaVinci Resolve for video editing, and Visual Studio for software development.

This isn't a limitation; it's a conscious choice. No cracked licenses exist on my system, and no project is ever delivered with stolen software.

The final quality of a piece depends on the skill, eye, and experience of the designer, not the brand and price of the software. An SVG, PNG, or final render meets the same global standard — and in many cases, files produced with free tools are actually more editable and more durable.

No cracked licenses

I don't use pirated software, and I don't ask you to use it for your project either.

Cleaner income

Work built with free and legal tools respects the creator's rights — and is ethically cleaner for you as well.

Quality, not the tool's name

The difference is in the output, not the logo at the top of the window. The result is what you see and touch.

Independence and file durability

Standard output files remain editable forever — with no dependency on subscriptions or licenses.
